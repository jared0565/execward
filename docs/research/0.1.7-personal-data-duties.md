# Task 0.1.7: personal data duties

Answered 2026-11-22 from the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) guidance, fetched by a
web-reading agent whose quotes I have not re-read myself. The ICO marks its DPIA guidance as
under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act. This is not legal advice.

## Answer

**Do a DPIA before any client personal data goes through ExecWard. It is not needed for
Phase 1**, which reads only the owner's own repositories.

**Provider terms are needed, and the API terms supply them.** Consumer plans do not (task 0.1.6).

## Is a DPIA required?

Probably, and the ICO's advice is to do one either way.

- The ICO's list requires a DPIA for innovative technology, which it says includes artificial
  intelligence, "when combined with any other criterion". AI alone is not an automatic trigger.
- Its AI guidance says: "In the vast majority of cases, the use of AI will involve a type of
  processing likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, and will
  therefore trigger the legal requirement for you to undertake a DPIA."
- Where no rule forces one: "If in any doubt, we would always recommend that you do a DPIA."
- If you decide against one, "you should document your reasons".
- One point in the owner's favour: "Individual professionals processing patient or client data
  are not processing on a large scale."

## What else must be in place

| Duty | What the ICO says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Written contract with each processor | "Whenever a controller uses a processor, there must be a written contract." | Both providers' API terms include a data processing addendum. Whether each meets every required clause was not checked clause by clause. |
| Safeguard for sending data to the US | Every restricted transfer needs adequacy rules, a safeguard such as the UK Addendum, or an exception. The rules cover sole traders and small, infrequent transfers. | Both addenda include the UK Addendum. |
| Transfer risk assessment | "If you rely on a safeguard, you must also ensure that you have completed a transfer risk assessment." | Not done. |
| Processors named in the DPIA | The DPIA should "include any processors involved". | Anthropic, OpenAI, Cloudflare and GitHub. |

## Open points

- **Your role with each client.** If a client is the controller and you are their processor,
  the client's contract decides whether you may pass their data to a model provider at all.
  That is a fact about each engagement, not something guidance can answer.
- **The UK-US data bridge.** Not checked for either provider. If one is certified, the transfer
  rests on adequacy and the risk assessment is not needed for it.
- **Cloudflare and GitHub** process the same data and need the same checks. Not read here.

These are tracked as task G.11.
